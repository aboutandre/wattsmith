# v0.15.0 — Pulse Hold also covers slower bursts and PV charging

Pulse Hold was built for the induction hob: ~1 kW switching every 3.5 s while the batteries discharge. On 25 Sep the washing machine, mixer and stove ran together while the sun was charging the batteries. The load jumped by 1.4–2.5 kW for 5–15 s every 11–34 s. The batteries need ~7 s to react, so every burst was imported and the late reaction then exported. Pulse Hold didn't step in, for two reasons: it looked for pulsing over only 30 s, and it never acts while the batteries charge.

**What changed**
- **Slower bursts are recognised.** Pulsing is now detected over 60 s (3 up/down swings of 300 W or more), and the hold looks back 30 s. Bursts every 15–30 s are caught as well as the hob.
- **New: hold while charging.** When the batteries charge from solar during such bursts, the charge rate is held at its lowest of the last 30 s, so solar covers the bursts instead of the grid. This only happens while the **remaining sun still fills the batteries to their ceiling with ~2 kWh to spare**. The solar held back would then have been exported at the top of the charge anyway, so nothing is lost. The check runs every tick, so a long wash that uses up the margin ends the hold and charging resumes.
- **Fix: stored energy in surplus alone no longer stops solar charging.** Before, a burst that briefly needed discharge while the sun was charging raised the battery output and stopped charging entirely. With the wider window this would have happened far more often. Now, whenever solar is charging, only the charge-side hold (with its fill check) can act.

**On the Pulse Hold switch:** new attributes `pv_fills_fleet` (is the charging check open?) and `hold_mode` (`discharge`, `charge` or none). `holding_w` is negative when the charge rate is capped.

**Replay through the real controller** (25 Sep burst pattern, solar charging): grid import 436 → 43 Wh per hour of bursts, at the cost of ~1.3 kWh/h less charging, which is why it waits for the fill check. The hob case is unchanged: 183 → 0 Wh/h import.

On 25 Sep itself the check would have stayed closed: the batteries were at ~33% and the remaining sun wouldn't fill them. Keeping that charge is worth more (~30–40 ct/h later) than the ~10 ct/h of import it would save. So on days like that the flip-flop remains the cheaper option.

296 tests pass.
