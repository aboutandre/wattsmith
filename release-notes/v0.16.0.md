# v0.16.0 — 80 % becomes a soft cap, and the house load is learned per 15 minutes

**Maximum Charge SOC is now a soft cap.**
The 80 % cap still spares the cells the top band most of the time. But on an afternoon like 26 Sep, the sun alone fills the batteries to 80 % before the cheap window ends. There's then no room to buy 14 ct energy for a 33–42 ct night, and Wattsmith bought nothing. Now the planner also works out what it would buy with the ceiling at the **Adaptive Ceiling SOC** (100 %). If that saves **at least 5 ct over the coming day**, it lifts the cap. It stays lifted while the saving is still at least 1 ct, so it doesn't switch back and forth. On 26 Sep it would have lifted and bought ~2.6 kWh at 14.1 ct for a 33 ct night. Free solar alone never lifts it, because the cells should only sit high when the energy is needed the same night. The EV solar right-of-way still wins. The Arbitrage Plan sensor shows `ceiling_lifted` and `ceiling_lift_gain_ct`.

**The house load is learned from your history, per weekday and 15 minutes.**
The planner and the adaptive PV gate used an hourly estimate. It fell back to the configured **Adaptive Baseline Load** (500 W) for unlearned hours, and the planner applied *today's* weekday to all of tomorrow. Wattsmith now fits a profile from the history DB (the exact energy used every 15 minutes) every 6 hours. It uses the last 8 weeks, with recent weeks counting more, and a separate profile per weekday where your data supports one. Tested on your 85 days (each day predicted only from the days before it):

| forecast | error over 6-hour windows | overnight error / bias |
|---|---|---|
| fixed 500 W (old fallback) | 32 % | 33 % / +32 % |
| hourly learner | 14 % | 9.5 % / −4.9 % |
| **new profile** | **13 %** | **8.1 % / −3.4 %** |

The adaptive PV gate now uses the profile's actual energy until sunset, instead of the current hour's load times the hours left. **Adaptive Baseline Load** is now only a fallback for the first week. A "same-day correction" (scaling the next hours by how today is running) was tested and made the forecast worse, so it isn't used.

**On the −60 W grid target:** the Tibber Pulse (the billing meter) reads **~14 W more import** than the Shelly around zero. So −60 W on the Shelly is about −46 W of real export. The Shelly's *energy counters* count each phase separately, so they show 2.5–2.9× the meter's import and export. Its *summed power*, which is what Wattsmith uses, matches the meter to within that 14 W.

The number entities **Maximum Charge SOC**, **Adaptive Baseline Load** and **Target Grid Power** now explain themselves in their `purpose` attribute.
