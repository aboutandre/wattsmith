# v0.15.2 — no more running empty before sunrise after a cheap afternoon

On 26 Sep there was grid energy at 14 ct all afternoon, yet the batteries were empty by 06:00 and the house bought ~1.2 kWh at 32–34 ct until the sun came back. Two things went wrong, and both are fixed.

**1. Wattsmith thought the batteries held more energy than they do.**
It turned the displayed SOC into energy using the rated capacity: 154 Wh per % for 3 × 5.12 kWh. Your history shows the batteries really deliver about **127 Wh per displayed %** to the house (a factor of 0.82–0.84, the same on all three), because of discharge losses and the battery's SOC reading drifting between full charges. On top of that, while the batteries cover the house, the zero-grid control sends about **85 W to the grid** (the −60 W target plus overshoot when loads switch off): 1.2 kWh that night. Together, the planner saw the night about 2 kWh richer than it was, decided there was "no deficit", and bought nothing.

Wattsmith now **measures both from your own history** every six hours and plans with them. Before there's enough history it starts from cautious values: √(round-trip efficiency) and the configured grid target. With the corrected numbers, a replay of 26 Sep buys ~2 kWh at 14 ct that afternoon and never runs empty.

**2. A calibration top-up stopped halfway for a 0.8 ct saving.**
The overdue SOC calibration was charging from the grid at 14.3 ct when tomorrow's prices were published at 13:00. Tomorrow's noon was 13.4 ct, so it stopped at 61 % to finish a day later, giving up cheap energy for a 33–42 ct evening and night. A top-up that has started now **finishes**, unless a later window is **more than 3 ct/kWh cheaper** *and* nothing in between costs more than the energy bought now would deliver at. A new top-up still starts in the cheapest window, as before.

**New attributes on the Arbitrage Plan sensor:** `delivery_factor`, `delivery_source` and `discharge_overhead_w`, `discharge_overhead_source` (`measured` or `seed`). They show what Wattsmith currently counts with.

**Worth knowing:** those ~85 W sent to the grid are 16–17 % of everything the batteries discharge, sold at the feed-in price. **Target Grid Power** (−60 W) controls most of it; a smaller value, such as −20 W, would keep more of it in the batteries.

318 tests pass, including a replay of 26/27 Sep built from the real data.
