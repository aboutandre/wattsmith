# v0.14.0 — the discharge hold keeps energy for the dearest hours

When the batteries can't cover everything until the next cheap window or the next sunny spell, Wattsmith now saves stored energy for the most expensive hours. It no longer spends that energy on whatever comes first.

**What changed**
- **Holding through a cheaper stretch.** Say a 34 ct night comes before a 45 ct morning. The old hold only kept energy for later when *buying* grid energy now would also have paid off, and 34 ct × losses + wear never beats 45 ct. So the batteries ran empty overnight and the house imported at the morning peak. The price you paid for energy already in the batteries is sunk, so keeping it is worthwhile whenever the later hour is simply more expensive. Now it is kept.
- **No more dropping the hold once the batteries hold "enough".** Energy bought cheaply for an evening peak used to be spent on cheap hours in between as soon as the forecast showed no shortfall. Stored energy is now set aside for each hour in order of how much it saves there. The hold keeps only what the more expensive hours actually depend on: an evening that midday solar will cover needs none of it.
- **The hold also applies when there is nothing to buy**, so a load above the forecast can't eat energy meant for a later peak.

Nothing to configure: **Arbitrage Discharge Hold SOC** works as before, it just holds at the right times now. On a night with a cheaper stretch before an expensive morning, it should stay up overnight and drop when the peak starts.

**Simulated results** (old vs new planner):
- One night with a 36 ct shoulder before a 45 ct morning: battery use overnight 1.43 → 0.44 kWh, grid import at the peak 1.56 → 0.25 kWh.
- A simulated winter (24 weeks, October–March, realistic forecast errors): grid cost **−3.2%** (about €13), and energy bought at ≥ 50 ct with empty batteries **39 → 15 kWh**.

The simulator's reference "best possible plan" could not hold energy either, which is why this gap went unnoticed. That is fixed too, and 25 new 15-minute test scenarios cover this case. 288 tests pass.
